# 03: Voice and Personality

*How the newsletter sounds. Use this for every piece of writing: issues, Instagram teasers, game questions. Update it when we find phrasing readers love or hate.*

## In one line

A smart senior student explaining the week's AI news to a friend: informed, honest, a little funny, never hype.

## Hard rules: never do these

These apply to every word we publish, including headlines, captions, quotes, game questions and social posts. No exceptions.

1. **No em dashes (—).** Never use them. Use a period, comma, colon or parentheses instead. For number ranges, write "10 to 15" or "10-15" with a plain hyphen.
    - Wrong: "The model is fast — and it's free."
    - Right: "The model is fast, and it's free."
2. **No cursing, ever.** No swear words of any strength, including censored ones (f\*\*\*), abbreviations (wtf, af) or "mild" ones. If a source we quote curses, paraphrase it instead of quoting.

**Final check before publishing:** search the issue for "—" and remove every one, then reread for any profanity.

## Personality

| We are | We are not |
| --- | --- |
| Curious | Hype-driven |
| Clear | Jargon-heavy |
| Honest and a bit skeptical | Doom-and-gloom or cheerleaders |
| Friendly, lightly playful | Cringe or meme-overloaded |
| Practical | Abstract |
| Confident | Know-it-all |

## Writing rules

1. **Lead with the point.** The first sentence of every item is bold and says what happened.
2. **Short.** Sentences under 20 words, paragraphs of 1 to 3 sentences.
3. **Talk to the reader.** "You" for the reader, "we" for the team.
4. **Explain jargon inline**, once: "fine-tuning (teaching an existing model a new skill with extra examples)".
5. **Numbers beat adjectives.** "40% faster", not "much faster".
6. **Claims are claims.** If only the company says it, write "says" or "claims", and link the source.
7. **Always a "so what".** Every story ends with why a student should care.
8. **Humor:** at most one light joke per section, never at the expense of victims or real people's misfortune.
9. **Emojis:** only as the fixed category tags [TO CONFIRM], never sprinkled through sentences.

## Words and phrases we avoid

"revolutionary", "game-changer", "mind-blowing", "insane", "in today's fast-paced world", "delve", "unlock the power of", "buckle up", "the future is here", and ALL-CAPS excitement.

## Before / after

**Too hype:**
> This lab just CHANGED EVERYTHING with its insane new model 🤯🔥

**Too dry:**
> The company has announced the release of a new large language model featuring improved performance across a range of benchmarks.

**Our voice:**
> **A major lab released a new model that scores 15% higher on coding tests than its last one.** If you use AI to debug assignments, expect fewer wrong fixes, but keep testing everything yourself.

## Headlines

Verb + specific detail. Under 12 words.

- Good: "A laptop-sized model now matches last year's best at coding"
- Bad: "Big news in open-source AI!"

## Sensitive stories (AI Gone Wrong)

State the facts calmly, name the source, avoid graphic detail, and never mock the people affected. End with what students can learn or do (e.g. how to spot a deepfake).
